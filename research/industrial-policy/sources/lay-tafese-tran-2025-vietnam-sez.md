# Lay, Tafese & Tran (2025), "From fields to factories: Special economic zones, foreign direct investment, and labour markets in Vietnam"

## Bibliographic info

- Authors: Jann Lay, Tevin Tafese, Van Tran
- Journal: *Journal of Development Economics* 174 (2025): 103482
- ScienceDirect: <https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0304387825000185>
- RePEc: <https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/deveco/v174y2025ics0304387825000185.html>
- SSRN preprint: <https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4631451>
- VoxDev summary: <https://voxdev.org/topic/labour-markets/role-special-economic-zones-vietnams-economic-success>

## Why it's the key Vietnam-SEA-SEZ citation

This is the closest 2020s analogue to Lu/Wang/Zhu (2019) for a Southeast
Asian setting. The paper anchors the "SEA is doing what early China did"
claim with comparable identification, comparable outcomes, and a similar
DiD-with-continuous-treatment design.

## Methodology

- District-year panel of Vietnam labour-force survey data, 2009–2019
  (some materials cite 2013–2019).
- Continuous SEZ-exposure measure constructed from **historical
  satellite imagery** of SEZ built-up area over time.
- Difference-in-differences with continuous treatment.

## Verbatim abstract (recovered)

> "Vietnam's deep integration into GVCs has been driven by the
> establishment of special economic zones (SEZs). This paper examines
> the local labour market impacts of this SEZ programme. We trace the
> built-up area of SEZs over time to construct a novel continuous
> measure of SEZ exposure using historical satellite imagery. In a
> difference-in-differences design with continuous treatment, we examine
> the impact of SEZ exposure on local labour market outcomes at the
> district-level for the period 2009–2019. We find that the expansion
> of SEZs has led to a rapid shift in employment from own-account and
> family work in agriculture to manufacturing in foreign firms. We also
> show that SEZ exposure leads to higher wages and more formal
> employment contracts. Earnings improvements are not confined to
> workers in foreign manufacturing firms, but spill over to workers in
> household businesses and domestic firms. The effects on women account
> for most of these impacts."

## Key findings (qualitative — quantitative magnitudes are paywalled)

1. **Labour reallocation.** Rapid shift from own-account / unpaid family
   work in agriculture and household businesses → wage employment in
   foreign manufacturing firms. This is the textbook Lewis transition
   captured in real time.
2. **Wage and formality effects.** SEZ exposure raises wages and the
   share of workers on formal contracts.
3. **Spillovers.** Earnings gains extend beyond foreign-firm employees
   to workers in household businesses and domestic firms — the local
   labour market clears at higher wages once the SEZ sector becomes a
   large enough buyer.
4. **Heterogeneity.** Effects are concentrated on **women**, on workers
   with **medium levels of education**, and on **rural** locations. The
   gender result is consistent with the global garments-and-electronics
   pattern (Bangladesh, Cambodia) and is one of the strongest
   distributional reasons SEZs have a poverty-reduction rather than just
   a wage-bill effect.

## Implications for the analysis

- This paper is the empirical bridge for the "early China vs SEA today"
  framing in `analysis.md` §3 and §4. Lu/Wang/Zhu measured the wage
  premium for early-Chinese coastal zones; Lay/Tafese/Tran measure the
  same kind of premium for contemporary Vietnamese zones, with similar
  methods and qualitatively similar results.
- The female-concentrated gains are not present in the Lu/Wang/Zhu
  framing of Chinese SEZs, where the early-coastal labour force was
  more mixed and where the gender story arrives via "left-behind
  children" rather than via female employment expansion. SEA's female-
  intensive SEZ workforce is a meaningfully different distributional
  channel.
- The spillover finding answers an objection that SEZs only help "the
  workers inside the fence" — they do not.
