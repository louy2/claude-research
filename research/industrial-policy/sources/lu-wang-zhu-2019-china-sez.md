# Lu, Wang & Zhu (2019), "Place-Based Policies, Creation, and Agglomeration Economies: Evidence from China's Economic Zone Program"

## Bibliographic info

- Authors: Yi Lu, Jin Wang, Lianming Zhu
- Journal: *American Economic Journal: Economic Policy* 11(3): 325–360
- Year: 2019
- Open mirror (HKUST CEP): <https://cep.hkust.edu.hk/sites/default/files/publications_media/full_paper/Place-Based%20Policies,%20Creation,%20and%20Agglomeration%20Economies%20Evidence%20from%20China%E2%80%99s%20Economic%20Zone%20Program.pdf>
- AEA landing: <https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257/pol.20160272>

## Why it's the key China-SEZ wage-effect citation

This is the cleanest causal identification of what coastal Chinese SEZs
*did* to local labour markets. Most of the popular discourse on Shenzhen
is a-causal aggregate description; Lu/Wang/Zhu identify the policy
effect using firm- and zone-level variation.

## Key findings (as recovered)

- Establishing an SEZ has positive effects on **capital investment,
  employment, output, productivity, and wages**, and increases the
  number of firms in the designated areas.
- **Wage effect: workers in SEZs see ~8% higher wages than the control
  group, against ~5% higher local cost of living** — i.e., a real wage
  gain of roughly 3 percentage points net of inflation, on top of the
  composition effect of moving from agricultural to industrial work.
- **Net entry of new firms** drives most of the effect; incumbent
  expansion is secondary. This is consistent with the Fernandes–Reed
  story that parks fix coordination failures rather than directly
  subsidising existing firms.
- **Capital-intensive industries benefit more than labour-intensive
  ones** from the zone programme — a finding that complicates the
  "parks lift the bottom of the labour market" narrative for *late*
  Chinese SEZs.

## Implications for the China-SEA comparison

- The Lu/Wang/Zhu wage premium is the *additional* gain from SEZ
  treatment on top of the much larger structural wage premium of
  rural-to-urban migration. The headline poverty story rests mostly on
  the latter, but the SEZ premium is the part attributable to policy
  rather than to migration alone.
- The capital-intensity skew is one mechanism behind the post-2010
  attenuation: as Chinese SEZs upgraded technologically, the marginal
  job created moved up the skill ladder, away from the rural
  surplus-labour pool that the early-coastal phase absorbed.
